1. The Foundation & The Rules

The Core Definitions

  • Sample Space (S): The collection of ALL possible outcomes. Example: Rolling a die → S = {1,2,3,4,5,6}.
  • Event (E): Any collection of outcomes from the experiment. Example: Rolling an even number → E = {2,4,6}.

The Two Immutable Rules of Probability

Rule 1: 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1
Rule 2: Σ P(e) = 1
  • Rule 1: Probability is ALWAYS between 0 (impossible) and 1 (certain). Negative probabilities do not exist.
  • Rule 2: If you add up the probability of every single outcome in the sample space, it must equal exactly 1.

Empirical vs. Classical Methods

  • Empirical (Experimental): Based on observing reality.
    P(E) ≈ (Frequency of E) / (Total Trials)
  • Classical (Theoretical): Based on equally likely outcomes (like a fair coin or die).
    P(E) = N(E) / N(S) (Number of ways E can happen divided by total possible outcomes).

2. The "OR" Protocol (Addition)

Disjoint (Mutually Exclusive) Events

Two events are disjoint if they have NO outcomes in common. (e.g., You cannot roll a 2 AND a 5 at the same time on a single die).

If Disjoint: P(E or F) = P(E) + P(F)

The General Addition Rule

If events ARE NOT disjoint (they overlap), you must subtract the overlap so you don't double-count it.

General: P(E or F) = P(E) + P(F) - P(E and F)

The Complement Rule

The complement ($E^c$) is everything that is NOT $E$. If the probability of rain is 30%, the probability of NOT rain is 70%.

P(E^c) = 1 - P(E)

3. The "AND" Protocol (Multiplication)

Independent Events

Two events are independent if event E happening does NOT affect the probability of event F happening. (e.g., Flipping a coin, then rolling a die).

If Independent: P(E and F) = P(E) × P(F)

You can chain this: $P(E \text{ and } F \text{ and } G) = P(E) \times P(F) \times P(G)$

The "At Least One" Trick

Calculating "at least one" directly is mathematically exhausting. Instead, calculate the probability that it happens ZERO times, and subtract it from 1.

P(At least one) = 1 - P(None)

4. The "GIVEN" Protocol (Conditional)

Conditional Probability

The probability of Event F occurring, GIVEN that Event E has already occurred. This physically shrinks your sample space down to only the outcomes where E happened.

P(F | E) = P(E and F) / P(E)
-- OR --
P(F | E) = N(E and F) / N(E)
  • The denominator is always the probability (or count) of the GIVEN condition.
  • Used heavily in Contingency Tables (Rows and Columns).
